The best budgeting appswithout open banking

By the Vero team•Updated 24 November 2026•6 min read

We make Vero, one of the apps in this guide. Nobody paid to be included, and we’ve tried to be fair: every app gets the same questions, and we say where others do things better. Prices and features checked on 24 November 2026. Spot something out of date? Email vero.fincorp@gmail.com.

Short answer

If you don’t want to connect your bank, these are the options worth a look in the UK:

  • Vero for fast logging by chat or voice, with challenges and rewards to keep you going.
  • Goodbudget for classic envelope budgeting, on your own or as a couple.
  • YNAB for strict zero-based budgeting, if you’ll pay for it and put in the time.
  • Spendee for a free manual tracker that splits money into wallets.
  • Fudget for the simplest possible list of money in and out.
  • A spreadsheet if you want full control and don’t mind building it.

Why budget without open banking?

Open banking lets an app read your bank transactions automatically. It’s regulated and widely used, but it isn’t for everyone. Our guide to open banking safety covers how it works.

People skip it for a few common reasons:

  • Privacy. You’d rather an app only knows what you choose to tell it.
  • Cash and shared money. Cash, split bills and money from family don’t show up neatly in a bank feed.
  • Attention. Logging a spend yourself makes you notice it. Automatic feeds are easy to ignore.
  • Your bank isn’t supported, or you don’t want to reconfirm access every 90 days.

The trade-off is effort. Without a bank feed, the app only knows what you tell it, so the best of these apps make logging as quick as possible.

The apps in detail

1Vero

Free · Plus £4.99/month
Best for: building a daily habit without a bank link

Vero is our app, so read this one with that in mind. You log a spend by texting Verdi, Vero’s elephant companion, or by voice. Vero Plus can log for you by reading your payment notifications, still without open banking.

You set a monthly plan around your payday, put money aside in pockets, and get a safe-to-spend number for each day. Challenges and streaks earn tokens, which unlock discounted gift cards.

Good

  • Never connects to your bank
  • Logging a spend takes seconds
  • Tag each spend as a need, want or impulse
  • Rewards for sticking to your plan, not for spending

Watch out

  • On the free plan, it only knows what you log
  • Auto-logging needs Plus and notifications switched on
  • No balances or net-worth view
  • Newer than the others here

2Goodbudget

Free · Premium $10/month
Best for: envelope budgeting, alone or with a partner

Goodbudget is a digital version of the cash-envelope method. You split your income into envelopes like rent, food and fun, then take money out of each one as you spend.

The free plan covers 10 regular envelopes, 10 for goals and yearly costs, one account and two devices. Bank sync on Premium only works with US banks, so in the UK you’ll be typing everything in.

Good

  • A proven, simple method
  • Free plan is genuinely usable
  • Easy to share with a partner

Watch out

  • All manual in the UK
  • Free plan keeps one year of history
  • Prices in US dollars

3YNAB

$14.99/month or $109/year
Best for: committed zero-based budgeters

YNAB (You Need A Budget) gives every pound a job before you spend it, until there’s nothing left to assign. It has a loyal following and lots of learning material.

You can link select UK banks, but you don’t have to: accounts can be tracked by hand. It’s the priciest app here and has no free plan after the 34-day trial. Read our Vero vs YNAB comparison for more.

Good

  • Very thorough method
  • Works with or without a bank link
  • Long free trial, no card needed

Watch out

  • Takes time to learn
  • No free plan
  • Priced in US dollars, plus tax

4Spendee

Free · Plus £3.99/month
Best for: a free manual tracker with wallets

Spendee lets you track spending by hand in wallets, such as cash, a holiday fund or a shared flat kitty. The free plan includes manual entries, one cash wallet, an overall budget and CSV export.

Plus (£3.99 a month or £29.99 a year) adds unlimited wallets, budgets and shared wallets. Bank connection is a separate Premium tier, so you can stay manual for good.

Good

  • Useful free plan
  • Shared wallets on Plus
  • GBP pricing

Watch out

  • Free plan has one wallet
  • Bank sync costs extra if you later want it

5Fudget

Free · Plus $19.99/year
Best for: the simplest list of money in and out

Fudget is deliberately basic: you make a budget, add what comes in and goes out, and see what’s left. There are no charts to learn and no bank connection on its site.

The free plan allows five budgets, 250 entries and one device. Plus removes the limits and syncs across devices.

Good

  • Almost nothing to learn
  • Cheap yearly plan
  • No bank connection at all

Watch out

  • Few insights or reports
  • Free plan has an entry limit
  • Prices in US dollars

6A spreadsheet

Free
Best for: people who like full control

Google Sheets or Excel, plus a simple template, can do everything a budgeting app does. You decide the categories, formulas and layout.

The catch is that it’s easy to stop. There are no reminders, and logging on your phone is fiddly.

Good

  • Free and flexible
  • Your data stays in your file

Watch out

  • You build and fix it yourself
  • Hard to keep up on the go

If you keep forgetting to trackVero

Logging by chat or voice takes seconds, and streaks give you a reason to come back.

If you budget with a partnerGoodbudget

Shared envelopes keep you both working from the same numbers.

Both have usable free plans without any bank connection.

Tips to make manual tracking stick

  • Log it straight away. A spend logged at the till takes five seconds. One remembered on Sunday takes five minutes, if you remember it at all.
  • Use fewer categories. Six to eight is plenty. You can always split one later.
  • Round the numbers. £3.20 or £3 makes no real difference to your month.
  • Do a weekly check-in. Ten minutes on Sunday to catch anything you missed.
  • Don’t restart, resume. Missed a few days? Log what you remember and carry on.

Questions people ask

Are budgeting apps without open banking safer?

They share less. An app that never connects to your bank can’t see your balances or transactions, so there’s less data to protect.

Open banking itself is regulated and read-only, so it isn’t unsafe. It’s a question of how much you want to share.

Can I use YNAB in the UK without linking my bank?

Yes. YNAB lets you add accounts and enter transactions by hand. You can also link select UK banks if you change your mind.

Is manual budgeting worth the effort?

For many people, yes. Typing a spend makes you notice it, which is often the point. The key is an app that makes logging fast enough to keep doing.

How does Vero log spending without open banking?

On the free plan, you tell Verdi what you spent by chat or voice. Vero Plus can also read your payment notifications on your phone and log them for you. It never logs into or connects to your bank.
