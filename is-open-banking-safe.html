Is open banking safefor budgeting apps?

By the Vero team•Updated 24 November 2026•4 min read

Short answer

Generally, yes. Open banking in the UK is regulated, you never give an app your bank password, and budgeting apps get read-only access that you can switch off at any time.

The bigger question is usually not the connection itself, but what the app does with your data once it has it.

How open banking works

Open banking lets you give a regulated app permission to see your bank account data. Instead of sharing your login, you approve access inside your own bank’s app or website.

Apps that only read your data are called account information services. Apps that make payments for you need a separate permission each time, which you approve with your bank.

What an app can and can’t do

See your transactions, including past onesYes
See account details like your sort codeYes

Exactly what’s shared depends on the permissions you approve, which your bank shows you before you agree.

How UK rules protect you

  • Regulated providers. Apps that access your account data must be authorised or registered with the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), or work as an agent of a firm that is.
  • No password sharing. You log in with your bank, not with the app.
  • Your consent, regularly checked. Since 2022, apps must ask you to reconfirm their access every 90 days.
  • You can switch it off. You can remove access in the app, and many banks let you do it from their app too.

Check an app before you connect. Search for the company on the FCA register. If it isn’t there, and isn’t an agent of a firm that is, don’t connect it.

The real risks

The open banking connection is the well-protected part. The risks sit around it.

  • What the app does with your data. Some apps analyse your spending to suggest products, and may earn money if you take them out. Read the privacy policy.
  • Data breaches. Any company holding your data could be hacked. A read-only breach can’t move money, but your transactions are personal.
  • Fake apps and scams. Scammers may pose as a well-known app. Only download from the official app stores and check the developer name.
  • Older connections. A few services still ask for your bank login directly, known as screen scraping. Avoid giving any app your bank password.

Before you connect: a quick checklist

  • The company is on the FCA register, or is an agent of a firm that is.
  • You were sent to your bank’s own app or site to approve access.
  • You understand what the app will see, and for how long.
  • The privacy policy says how your data is used and whether it’s shared.
  • You know how to remove access later.

How to switch off an app’s access

You can stop sharing at any time. Usually there are two ways:

  • In the budgeting app: look for “connected accounts” or similar in settings, and disconnect the bank.
  • In your bank’s app: many banks list connected third-party apps in their settings, where you can remove access.

Removing access stops new data being shared. To delete what the app already holds, ask the app to delete your data under UK data protection law.

Prefer not to share? There are other ways

Open banking is one way to feed a budgeting app, not the only one. Some apps work with what you tell them, or with what your payment notifications say.

Questions people ask

Can a budgeting app take money from my account?

Not with read-only access. Budgeting apps use account information access, which lets them see data but not move money. Payments need separate approval with your bank each time.

Why does my budgeting app ask me to reconnect every 90 days?

UK rules require apps to reconfirm your consent every 90 days. It’s a check that you still want to share your data.

Is open banking the same as giving an app my login?

No. With open banking, you log in with your bank and the app never sees your password. Be wary of any service that asks for your bank login directly.

How do I know if an app is regulated?

Search for the company on the FCA register. Regulated apps usually also name their FCA status at the bottom of their website.
