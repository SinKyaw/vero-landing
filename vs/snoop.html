A Snoop alternativethat never needs your bank

By the Vero team•Updated 24 November 2026•6 min read

Short answer

Snoop is one of the most useful free money apps in the UK. Connect your accounts and it looks for savings: bills going up, subscriptions you forgot, better deals to switch to.

Vero works on a different part of your money: the everyday spending you choose. It never connects to your bank. You log each spend in seconds, ask why you bought it, and build the habit with streaks and challenges.

Pick Snoop to cut your bills. Pick Vero to change your day-to-day spending. Many people would be better off with both.

What Snoop does well

  • It finds savings for you. Snoop tracks regular payments and subscriptions, reminds you about bills and contract renewals, and suggests deals to switch to.
  • A generous free plan. Monthly and category budgets, spending by merchant and category, a weekly spending report and daily money-saving tips are all free.
  • Your credit score. You can check it in the app for free.
  • Wide bank support. It connects to the major UK banks that support open banking, including Monzo, Revolut, Starling and the high-street banks.

If your main goal is to stop overpaying for bills and subscriptions, Snoop is built for that and Vero isn’t.

Cutting your bills vs changing your spending

Snoop is strongest on your fixed costs: the bills, contracts and subscriptions that leave your account every month. It reads your bank feed and tells you where you could pay less.

Vero is about the spending you decide on each day: lunch, a night out, a top that was on sale. That’s harder to fix with a better deal. It changes when you notice it. So Vero has no bank feed: you log each spend in a sentence as it happens, and a daily reflection asks whether it was a need, a want, an impulse or pressure.

The two don’t compete much. One trims what you pay for things you already have. The other changes what you buy next.

How Vero works instead

  • Log in one sentence. Type or say “coffee 3.20 and 12 for lunch at Pret” and Vero files both spends, with amount, place and category. You can also snap a receipt.
  • Log without opening the app. On iPhone, use Siri, the Action button or a Shortcut. On Android, Vero can offer to log the payment notifications from banking apps you choose. It reads the notification, never your bank account.
  • Ask yourself why. A daily reflection asks whether a spend was a need, a want, an impulse or pressure, and whether you’d buy it again.
  • Keep it going. Streaks, daily and weekly challenges and weekly leagues earn XP and tokens. Tokens send Verdi, Vero’s elephant, on trips that bring back discounts on gift cards from brands you already use.
  • Take your data with you. Export everything to an Excel workbook with live formulas, or your transactions to CSV.

Honestly, use both

This is one comparison where you don’t need to choose. Let Snoop watch your bills, subscriptions and switching. Use Vero for the everyday spending a better deal can’t fix.

The only overlap is budgets. If you set them in both apps, pick one to be the one you check, or the numbers will pull you in two directions.

Which one fits you

When Snoop is the better choice

  • You want your spending tracked automatically from your bank.
  • You want help cutting bills, subscriptions and contracts.
  • You want your credit score and a savings account in the same app.

When Vero is the better choice

  • You don’t want to connect your bank to any app.
  • Your money goes on everyday spending more than on bills.
  • You want to understand why you spend, not only what you spend.
  • You’re motivated by streaks, challenges and rewards.

Questions people ask

Is there anything Snoop does that Vero doesn’t?

Yes, quite a lot. Snoop tracks your accounts automatically, spots bills and subscriptions, offers switching deals, shows your credit score and has a savings account. Vero does none of these. It focuses on your everyday spending habit.

Does Snoop need my bank details?

Snoop says you need to connect at least one bank or credit card account to use it. It connects through open banking, so it doesn’t see your bank password. Vero never connects to your bank.

Is Snoop free?

Yes. The free version includes budgets, bill reminders and spending insights. Snoop Plus is £5.99 a month or £47.99 a year and adds things like custom categories, payday-to-payday tracking, manual accounts and CSV export.

Who owns Snoop?

Snoop joined Vanquis Banking Group in July 2023. Its company, Usnoop Limited, is registered with the Financial Conduct Authority for payment services.

Can I use Snoop and Vero together?

Yes, and they fit well. Use Snoop to keep an eye on bills, subscriptions and switching. Use Vero for the everyday spending you choose, like food, nights out and shopping.
