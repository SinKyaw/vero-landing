A Fortune City alternativewith real-world rewards

By the Vero team•Updated 24 November 2026•6 min read

Short answer

Fortune City is one of the best-known gamified money apps. Every time you log, your city grows. It’s charming, it’s well made, and it has made a lot of people enjoy budgeting.

Vero is gamified too, but its rewards reach outside the app. Tokens send Verdi, Vero’s elephant, on trips that bring back discounts on gift cards from real brands, and weekly leagues put you against people at your level.

Pick Fortune City if building the city is what keeps you logging. Pick Vero if you want the game to pay off in real life.

What Fortune City does well

  • It makes logging fun. Its developer calls it “a game that combines accounting with city simulation”. Record your spending and income, and watch your city grow.
  • Lots to collect. Over 100 building styles, plus vehicles, citizens and themes.
  • Friends. You can see your ranking among your friends.
  • Proper tracking underneath. Budgets, more than 10 categories, multiple accounts, currency exchange and pie and bar charts.
  • Loved by its users. It has 4.4 out of 5 from 896 ratings on the UK App Store.

Rewards inside the game vs rewards in real life

In Fortune City, the payoff for logging is the city: new buildings, happier citizens, a better-looking metropolis. That works if the city is what keeps you coming back.

Vero’s payoff is meant to leave the app. Logging and challenges earn tokens. Tokens send Verdi on trips, and he brings back discounts on gift cards from brands you already use. Spending more never earns you more: you’re rewarded for logging and checking in, not for buying things.

Vero also points the game at why you spend, not only at how often you log. A daily reflection asks whether a spend was a need, a want, an impulse or pressure, and whether you’d buy it again.

How Vero works instead

  • Log in one sentence. Type or say “coffee 3.20 and 12 for lunch at Pret” and Vero files both spends, with amount, place and category. You can also snap a receipt.
  • Log without opening the app. On iPhone, use Siri, the Action button or a Shortcut. On Android, Vero can offer to log the payment notifications from banking apps you choose. It reads the notification, never your bank account.
  • Ask yourself why. A daily reflection asks whether a spend was a need, a want, an impulse or pressure, and whether you’d buy it again.
  • Keep it going. Streaks, daily and weekly challenges and weekly leagues earn XP and tokens. Tokens send Verdi, Vero’s elephant, on trips that bring back discounts on gift cards from brands you already use.
  • See if it’s working. A one-minute weekly check-in tracks how you feel about money, on a research-based wellbeing scale.

Switch, or keep both?

If Fortune City keeps you logging every day, that’s the hard part done, and you may not need another app. If the city has stopped being a reason to open it, Vero gives you a different reason: real discounts, a weekly league and a streak to protect.

Running both means logging every spend twice, which few people keep up. Try Vero for a week alongside it and keep whichever you actually open.

Questions people ask

Is there anything Fortune City does that Vero doesn’t?

Yes. Fortune City turns logging into a city-building game, with over 100 building styles, and lets you rank against friends. It also runs on iPad. Vero’s leagues match you with people at your level rather than friends.

Is Fortune City free?

It’s free to download, with ads and in-app purchases. Its Chief Financial Officer subscription is £7.99 a month or £69.99 a year on the UK App Store, after a 14-day free trial.

Does Fortune City connect to my bank?

Its own pages describe adding transactions yourself and don’t mention bank connections. Vero never connects to your bank either.

Do gamified budgeting apps actually work?

They can help a habit stick, as long as the game rewards the right thing. Our guide to gamified budgeting apps covers what to look for.
